# Work Order WO-16 — pluf project (Feldman, Wilce)

**Paper V**: *Support families of closed subspaces of $\ell^2$, with an
application to diagonalizable projection filters*. This commission mechanizes
the verifiable core — the union-closure results, where closedness enters, and
the lattice sections, where the new mathematics lives and where the paper
connects to Papers I–IV.

**Ground truth:** this tarball (`pluf-wo16.tar.gz`). Contract:
`PlufWO16.lean`. Paper source: `paper5.pdf`, included. Also included:
`PAPERS.md` from the v1.0 release, which is the index from printed assertions
to Lean names for the existing development, and `REPORT-WO5.md` and
`REPORT-WO14.md`, whose signature tables cover the Gowers machinery and the
dependent-choice gadget this commission may reuse.

**Base:** the v1.0 release tree (243 theorems; CI runs #1–#14; full project
under `base/`). `PlufWO16.lean` imports `RequestProject.PlufWO15`. All 243
prior theorems must remain green; `PlufWO7a.lean` is the census record and is
not to be edited.

**Toolchain:** `leanprover/lean4:v4.28.0`, Mathlib pinned as included.

## Protocol (binding; as WO-1–WO-15)

Census first. Report-rather-than-repair with the codified license (false
contract ⇒ formalized counterexample + verbatim contract in comment + marked
minimal repair; ratification at audit). No `sorry`/`admit`/`axiom`/
`native_decide`; `#print axioms` per contract theorem; whitelist `propext`,
`Classical.choice`, `Quot.sound`. Partial order: B → C → A (Parts B and C are
the mathematically new material and depend only on existing machinery; Part A
is self-contained and carries the one hard analytic item).

## Scalars

Paper V is stated over $\C$, because its finite-rank classification is
representability and the theorem it invokes is field-sensitive. Everything
contracted here is field-blind, and the paper's conventions say so: its
Sections 7 and 8 use no property of the scalars. This commission therefore
mechanizes over $\R$, against the existing development. **If any contracted
item turns out to need a property of $\R$, that is a finding — report it**,
since the paper asserts the opposite.

## The census gate: Sections 4–6 are not contracted

Three parts of Paper V are deliberately excluded, and the census should return
a verdict on each rather than proofs:

1. **Scrawl axioms (§4).** Mathlib has a `Matroid` API with infinite ground
   sets. Does it support circuits, scrawls (unions of circuits), duals, and
   cofinitary matroids at reasonable cost? Would Theorem 4.3 of the paper
   (union closure plus finite-set elimination) be cheap on top of Part A?
2. **The finite-rank classification (§5).** This consumes the theorem of Aroca
   et al., which would enter as a quarantined hypothesis in the manner of
   Mathias and MSS. Is the transfer statement (Theorem 5.2) worth contracting
   against such a hypothesis, or is the hypothesis so close to the conclusion
   that the exercise is empty? Give an opinion.
3. **The model space (§6).** Generalized Vandermonde positivity, zero counts
   for exponential sums, and Blaschke products. Report what Mathlib has, if
   anything.

A clear verdict on these three is a primary deliverable, of the same standing
as the proofs.

## Item notes

**Part A.** A1's counting step is that a countable set of reals is not all of
$\R$. A2 is the one hard analytic item of the commission: the recursion with
frozen minima. Note that its stage constraint refers to the accumulated partial
sum, not only to earlier terms, so `PlufWO14.exists_seq_of_step` may not apply
directly; report the shape used. A3 reduces to A2 by choosing, for each point
of the union, one vector whose support contains it.

**Part B.** All of this is elementary given the existing block API. B2's
equality half is `PlufWO6.maximality_criterion`. B4's backward direction is the
proper-ideal-to-ultrafilter argument; the ideal generated by `cI M` consists of
the finite unions of its members precisely because `cI M` is downward closed
(B1).

**Part C.** C1–C3 are short. C4 is the substance: `PlufWO5/PartD.lean` proves
the finitely supported case of the pair-sum vanishing, and its engine is the
recursion `s k = ((k+2)/(k+1)) * s (k+1)` carried by the constraint vectors.
**Extract and export the general recursion** (item C4a, whose statement is left
open — supply it), then C4b and C4 follow. Note the indexing: the development
is 0-indexed and the paper displays pairs 1-indexed; reconcile once and report
the convention. C5 is two lines.

## Context

Papers I–IV are released with their verification (v1.0, 243 theorems). Paper V
is a companion: it supplies the combinatorial theory of the invariant that
Papers I–II use implicitly, and its Section 8 adds to Paper II a grading of
intimacy, a collapse theorem for plufs, the covering number of Gowers's
example, and a fourth constraint on the rigidity conjecture of Paper II §3.
Those four results are what this commission most needs to certify.
